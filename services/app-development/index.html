Common questions

Answers about app development. Can't find yours? Send us your question or read our guide on desktop apps vs web apps.

Which platforms can you build for?

Windows, macOS, iPhone (iOS) and Android. Many apps can share one codebase across platforms. When speed or a native feel matters most, we build natively. We recommend the best approach in your proposal.

How long does it take to build an app?

Fast. Most apps are delivered within a few days, and bigger apps usually take about 5 to 6 days, sometimes a little more or less. Mobile apps also go through App Store and Google Play review, which Apple and Google control and which usually takes from a day to a few days. We agree the exact timeline with you before we start.

How much does an app cost?

It depends on your budget and on your app, including the platforms, the features and the integrations. Start by filling in the project form and sharing your idea, even if it's rough. We'll reply with a free estimate, and after a short chat you get a fixed quote before any work starts. Developer accounts and store fees are paid by you directly.

Can you publish the app on the App Store and Google Play?

Yes. We prepare the store listing, screenshots and privacy details and handle the submission. The app is published under your own developer account, so it stays yours.

Can the app update itself?

Yes. Desktop apps get one-click automatic updates, the same approach our own Windows apps use. Mobile apps update through the App Store and Google Play.

Can you add licensing, subscriptions or in-app purchases?

Yes. We set up free trials, license keys, subscriptions and in-app purchases, connected to the payment method that suits your business and each store's rules.

Can you turn our website or Windows app into a Mac or mobile app?

Often, yes. We review what you already have and reuse as much as possible (the logic, the design or the backend) so the new app costs less and stays consistent.

Who owns the app and the code?

You do. The source code is in your repository, and the store accounts, signing certificates and backend are in your name.
