E-commerce website vs. Facebook page: which is better for selling online?

Selling through social media is a great start. Here's how it compares with your own online store, and the signs it's time to build one.

Updated October 29, 20263 min readZenra AI team

Many businesses start selling through a Facebook page, an Instagram profile or direct messages. It's free, fast and puts products in front of people who already follow you. But as orders grow, taking payments in chat, tracking stock in your head and relying on the algorithm starts to hold the business back.

Here's an honest comparison, and the signs it's time to open your own store.

Selling through a Facebook or Instagram page

What works well:

  • No setup cost, and you can start today.
  • Your audience is already there.
  • Conversations build trust, especially for new brands.

Where it struggles:

  • Orders arrive in messages and have to be handled one by one.
  • No real checkout, so payments, addresses and confirmations are manual.
  • Reach depends on an algorithm you don't control.
  • Little data about who buys and why, which makes ads harder to optimize.
  • The platform owns the relationship with your customers.

Selling through your own online store

What works well:

  • Customers can buy at any hour without waiting for a reply.
  • Card and wallet payments, shipping rates and order emails are automatic.
  • Product pages can rank in search engines and bring free traffic.
  • You own your customer list and can email past buyers.
  • Accurate tracking makes every advertising dollar easier to measure.

What to plan for:

  • A setup cost and usually a monthly platform fee.
  • Someone has to keep products, prices and apps up to date.
  • A store still needs traffic — social media and ads remain important.

Side-by-side comparison

ComparedFacebook or Instagram pageYour own online store
Cost to startFreeSetup plus monthly fees
Checkout and paymentsManual, in messagesAutomatic, 24/7
Search engine trafficMinimalYes, from product pages
Customer dataOwned by the platformYours

Signs it's time to build a store

  • You spend hours a day answering “price?” and taking orders in messages.
  • Orders get lost, duplicated or delayed.
  • Customers ask to pay by card or want an order confirmation.
  • You're running ads and can't tell which ones bring sales.
  • You have more than twenty or so products, or several variants per product.
  • You want to sell beyond your local area.

You don't have to choose

The strongest setup uses both. Social media brings discovery and conversation; the store handles checkout, payments and data. Link your social profiles to your store, and every post can send people to a product page that's ready to sell.

How to get started

  1. Choose a platform. Shopify is the easiest to run; WooCommerce gives more control if you already use WordPress.
  2. Set up the essentials. Payments, shipping zones, taxes, and clear policy pages for shipping, returns and privacy.
  3. Make great product pages. Good photos, honest descriptions, sizes and delivery times.
  4. Add tracking. Google Analytics 4 from day one, so you can see which channels bring sales.
  5. Connect your social channels so followers can shop in a tap.
